## 1. Mastery Percentage Thresholds Across Systems

### Common Core & State Standards
- **No single national threshold** — states set their own proficiency definitions
- **Trend (2011-2015):** 45 states raised proficiency standards
- **Most common threshold:** 80% for mastery-based systems
- **Advanced threshold:** 90% (some districts/states)

### AP College Board Standards
- **Mastery Definition:** Score of 3+ on 5-point scale (passing)
- **Target success rate:** 60-80% of test-takers achieving mastery (3+)
- **Top mastery (4-5):** ~10-30% of test-takers
- **This suggests:** In K12 systems with mixed-ability learners, 60-80% mandatory objective mastery = standard achievement

### K-12 Mastery-Based Learning Practices
- **Primary threshold:** 80% proficiency on learning standards
- **Advanced threshold:** 90% for stricter requirements
- **Key principle:** All students must demonstrate mastery before advancement
- **Common framework:** Reassessment allowed until mastery achieved

### Recommendation for Viridian
**Use 80% as the default threshold for standard mastery** (aligns with most K12 systems and AP expectations). Allow schools to configure 75-90% ranges per district policy.

## 2. Mandatory vs. Optional Objectives Impact on Mastery

### Proposed Framework
- **Mandatory Objectives (40-60% of total):** ALL must be mastered (≥80%) to pass course
- **Optional Objectives (40-60% of total):** Contribute to overall GPA/enrichment, not required for passing
- **Mastery Calculation:** 
  - If ANY mandatory objective < 80% → Course incomplete
  - Cumulative score = (Mandatory avg × 0.6) + (Optional avg × 0.4)

**Rationale:** Ensures students achieve core competencies while allowing flexibility for differentiation.

## 3. History Writing Rubric: High/Medium/Low Standards

### Writing Task Types in History Classes

#### A. Analytical Essay (Primary Assessment)
**Task:** Students write 3-5 page essay analyzing historical cause/effect, comparing periods, or evaluating historiographical arguments.

| Level | Thesis & Argument | Evidence & Sources | Historical Context | Analysis Quality | Organization |
|-------|-------------------|-------------------|-------------------|-----------------|--------------|
| **HIGH (90-100%)** | Clear, nuanced thesis with complexity; adjusted for evidence | 4+ credible sources; quotes integrated smoothly; sources evaluated critically | Accurate dates, figures, events; nuanced understanding of period complexities | Moves beyond summary; compares sources; synthesizes different perspectives; addresses historiography | Logical flow; smooth transitions; conclusion ties to thesis |
| **MEDIUM (75-89%)** | Clear thesis; some complexity present | 3-4 sources; adequate quotes; basic source evaluation | Mostly accurate context; understands main themes; some nuance | Combines summary with analysis; makes connections but may be surface-level | Generally organized; transitions present; conclusion present |
| **LOW (Below 75%)** | Weak/unclear thesis; restates prompt | 1-2 sources; insufficient quotes; no source evaluation | Vague or inaccurate context; basic understanding only | Heavy on summary; minimal analysis; restates facts | Disorganized or unclear flow; weak transitions; missing conclusion |

#### B. Document-Based Question (DBQ/Source Analysis)
**Task:** Analyze 4-6 primary/secondary sources, synthesize evidence, and answer historical question.

| Level | Source Analysis | Evidence Selection | Cross-document Connections | Historical Reasoning | Perspective Recognition |
|-------|-----------------|-------------------|---------------------------|---------------------|------------------------|
| **HIGH (90-100%)** | Identifies purpose, bias, context, audience; explains "why now" | Selects 5+ most relevant pieces; supports main argument | Finds patterns across ≥3 sources; explains why sources agree/disagree | Explains causal mechanisms; considers alternatives; addresses complexity | Recognizes multiple viewpoints; explains why different groups saw differently |
| **MEDIUM (75-89%)** | Identifies basic purpose and context; limited bias analysis | Selects 3-4 pieces; mostly relevant to prompt | Connects 2-3 sources; some cross-referencing | Makes causal claims; limited exploration of alternatives | Recognizes 2 viewpoints; basic explanation of differences |
| **LOW (Below 75%)** | Summarizes sources; minimal analysis | Uses 1-2 pieces or uses irrelevant sources | Lists sources separately; minimal connection | Focuses on "what happened" not "why"; no alternatives considered | Mentions one viewpoint or ignores perspective entirely |

#### C. Comparative Essay (Two Periods/Regions/Events)
**Task:** Compare and contrast two historical phenomena, explaining similarities and differences.

| Level | Comparison Framework | Supported Claims | Scope & Complexity | Analytical Depth | Use of Examples |
|-------|----------------------|-----------------|-------------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| **HIGH (90-100%)** | Clear criteria for comparison announced upfront; balanced treatment | All claims supported by evidence; acknowledges nuance | Considers multiple dimensions (political, social, economic, cultural) | Explains WHY similarities/differences occurred; causal reasoning | 4+ specific, relevant examples; specific dates/names |
| **MEDIUM (75-89%)** | Comparison criteria present; somewhat organized | Most claims supported; some unsupported assertions | Addresses 2-3 dimensions; some depth | Makes comparisons; limited explanation of causes | 2-3 examples; generally specific |
| **LOW (Below 75%)** | Mostly separate descriptions of each; minimal comparison | Weak support; general statements | Only one dimension addressed | "Just the facts" comparison; no deeper analysis | 0-1 example; vague or generic |

#### D. Short-Answer Response (5-15 minutes, 1-2 paragraphs)
**Task:** Quick assessment of specific skill (identify causes, define concept, explain event).

| Level | Accuracy | Completeness | Context | Language |
|-------|----------|-------------|---------|----------|
| **HIGH (90-100%)** | Completely accurate; no misconceptions | Answers all parts; adds relevant detail | Brief but meaningful historical context | Clear, concise, uses historical vocabulary appropriately |
| **MEDIUM (75-89%)** | Mostly accurate; minor errors don't undermine answer | Answers main parts; may miss some nuance | Basic context provided | Generally clear; mostly appropriate vocabulary |
| **LOW (Below 75%)** | Significant errors or misconceptions | Incomplete; addresses only one part | Minimal or incorrect context | Unclear or vague; misuse of terms |

#### E. Primary Source Analysis (Close Reading)
**Task:** Analyze excerpt from primary source (letter, speech, statute, etc.) for author intent, bias, intended audience.

| Level | Document Contextualization | Author Analysis | Bias & Perspective | Significance | Connections |
|-------|---------------------------|-----------------|-------------------|-------------|------------|
| **HIGH (90-100%)** | Accurate date, author, historical setting; "why this document mattered then" | Explains author's position, expertise, motivation; considers what author left out | Clearly identifies bias; explains HOW bias shaped the text; recognizes intended audience | Explains historical significance; why this source is important | Connects to larger historical theme or previous learning |
| **MEDIUM (75-89%)** | Basic historical info provided | Identifies who wrote it; some motivation explained | Names bias; limited explanation of impact on text | Explains why source matters but not deeply | Makes connection to unit theme |
| **LOW (Below 75%)** | Missing or inaccurate context | Only names author; no motivation | Mentions bias without analysis | "This shows what happened" but no deeper significance | No connections or vague connections |

## 4. Thresholds Summary for Implementation

### Mandatory Objective Mastery Levels
- **Standard Mastery:** 80% (aligns with K12 industry practice + AP standards)
- **Advanced Mastery:** 90%+ (for honors/accelerated tracks)
- **Proficient:** 75-79% (meets most standards but not full mastery)
- **Developing:** 60-74% (showing progress, needs reassessment)
- **Below Standard:** <60% (significant additional support needed)

### Sample Course Mastery Formula
```
Course Grade = (Mandatory Objectives Avg × 60%) + (Optional Objectives Avg × 40%)
- If ANY mandatory objective < 80% → Student cannot graduate course (flag for intervention)
- Reassessment window: Students get unlimited attempts to reach 80% on mandatory objectives
```
